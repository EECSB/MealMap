# MealMap — how to use it

Everything here happens in one page. There is no sign-up, nothing to install, and no server —
your recipes stay in your browser. Start by adding a couple of meals; the rest builds on that.

## The four tabs

| Tab | What it is for |
|---|---|
| 🍽️ **Menu** | Your recipes |
| 🥫 **Pantry** | What you currently have at home |
| 🛒 **Shopping** | What you need to buy |
| 📅 **Calendar** | What you are eating, and when |

⚙️ **Settings** is in the top right: language, units, the AI key, and where your data goes.

## Menu — your recipes

**Add a meal** with **＋ Add meal**. Only the name is required — fill in the rest whenever you like.

- **Photos.** Paste a link, upload from your computer, or drag an image straight in — including
  dragging one out of a web page. Add several; the first is the one shown on the card. Big photos are
  shrunk automatically so they do not fill up your browser's storage.
- **Ingredients and steps** are one per line. Put the quantity at the front — `200 g flour`,
  `2 cloves garlic` — and the rest of the app can then add them up for you.
- **Prep time, servings, nutrition and a link to the original** are all optional.

**Click a card** to open the recipe. From there you can:

- **Scale the servings** with **− / +**. Every quantity follows, and `500 g` tripled becomes
  `1.5 kg` rather than `1500 g`. This only changes what you are looking at — the saved recipe is
  never touched, so you can always press *reset* to go back.
- **★ Favourite it**, or mark it **cooked today** so you can see how long it has been.
- **⧉ Duplicate** it — the fastest way to make a variation without retyping. The copy opens straight
  away with its name selected.
- **📅 Add it to the calendar**, choosing the day and whether it is breakfast, lunch, dinner or a
  snack.

**Searching** covers names, descriptions, categories **and ingredients**. Type `mush` and you get
everything with mushrooms in it; when the match came from an ingredient rather than the title, the
card tells you which one, so a result is never a mystery. It copes with accents (`cebula` finds
`čebula`) and with different words for the same thing (`aubergine` finds `eggplant`).

Filter by category, or press **★** to show only your favourites. **🎲 Random suggestion** picks
something for you, with an **Another** button when you do not like the first answer.

## Pantry — what you have in

Add what you have at home. Quantity, category and emoji are all optional — a name is enough.

Two things then happen automatically:

1. Recipe ingredients you already own get a green **✓ in pantry** badge.
2. The shopping list leaves them out.

Mark something **Low** when you are running out; **Add low-stock** on the Shopping tab then adds
everything you have flagged in one go.

The matching is more forgiving than a plain word search: it understands plurals and word endings, so
*Eggs* covers *1 egg* and *sol* covers *soli*. It is also deliberately strict where it matters —
having **Milk** does **not** mean you have **buttermilk** or **coconut milk**, and having **Rice**
does not mean you have **rice vinegar**.

## Shopping — what to buy

Two panels.

**Your shopping list.** Type things in by hand, tick them off as you shop, and use **Clear checked**
when you get home.

**＋ From plan** is the useful one. Pick a range — this week, next week, the next 7 or 14 days, a
month, or your own dates — and it walks the calendar, collects every meal you have planned, drops
anything already in your pantry, and adds the rest.

It adds quantities up where it sensibly can: `2 cloves` + `3 cloves` becomes `5 cloves`, and
`200 g` + `1 kg` becomes `1.2 kg`. Where it cannot be sure — cloves of garlic plus a whole head, say
— it lists them separately rather than guess, because a wrong total is worse than two lines.

Press it twice and nothing is duplicated.

**Meals you can make** is the second panel: every recipe with a *have / total* score against your
pantry, what is missing, and **＋ Add missing** to put just those items on the list. Switch it to
**Ready to cook** to see only things you could make right now.

### ⤴ Share — getting the list out of the browser

The list is not much use on the computer you planned it on. **⤴ Share** opens a panel with the list
written out as plain text — exactly as it reads on screen, in the units you have chosen — and five
ways to take it with you:

| | |
|---|---|
| **⧉ Copy** | Onto the clipboard, to paste anywhere. |
| **📲 Send to an app…** | Your phone's or computer's own share sheet — WhatsApp, Messages, Notes. Only appears where the browser offers it, which in practice means phones and some desktops. |
| **⬇ Text file** | A `.txt` download. No length limit, and the one to use for a very long list. |
| **✉ Email** | See below. |
| **📱 QR code** | See below. |

Already-ticked items are left out, since you have bought them. Tick **Include items you have already
ticked off** and they come along with a ✓ instead of a dash.

**Email.** Type an address and press **Write the email**. This opens *your own* mail app with the
subject and the list already filled in, and you press send. MealMap has no server, so it cannot send
mail for you — and that is also why nothing leaves your device unless you send it yourself. The
address is remembered in this browser so mailing it to yourself is one click next time; it is never
part of an export or a sync.

**QR code.** Point another phone's camera at it and the list appears on that phone — no account, no
app, nothing typed, and no internet needed. The code is drawn on your own device, so the list is not
sent to any website to be turned into a picture. A very long list will not fit in a code a phone can
read; when that happens the panel says so and points you at the text file instead.

## Calendar — the week's plan

A month at a time, Mondays first. **Click any day** to add meals to it, or use **🎲 Surprise me** to
have one picked.

The day view shows your meals as a **gallery of cards** — photo, name and the one-line description —
so you can pick by looking rather than by remembering names. Tap a card to choose it, pick the slot,
and press **＋ Add**; tapping the chosen card again unpicks it. Once you have more than eight meals a
**search box** appears above the cards, and it searches ingredients as well as names — so "aubergine"
finds the recipe that uses one.

**Drag a meal to another day** to move it. On a computer just drag it; on a phone, **hold it for a
moment first**, then drag — that way an ordinary swipe still scrolls the page.

### Batch cooking

**🍲 Batch cook** in a recipe is for cooking once and eating it several times. Choose the day you are
cooking, how many meals it makes, and it fills in the following days for you — change any of them
before confirming.

Those days stay linked. The calendar shows the day you cook normally and the later ones as
**♻ leftovers**, so a glance at the month tells you which meals you actually have to stand at the
stove for. The shopping list is smart about this too: a batch is shopped for **once**, however many
days it covers.

Removing one leftover day just removes that day. **🍲 ✕** in the day view removes the whole run.

## Cook mode and printing

**▤ Cook mode** in a recipe gives you the whole screen: big text, just the ingredients and the steps,
nothing else. **Tap any line to strike it off** as you go, and the screen stays awake while you cook
so it does not lock with batter on your hands.

Whatever you scaled the servings to comes with you, and you can still change it mid-cook. Press
**Escape** or **✓ Done** to come back.

**🖨 Print** — or Ctrl-P with a recipe open — gives a clean one-page card: no photo, no buttons, black
on white.

> Keeping the screen awake needs Chrome, Edge, Firefox or a recent Android browser. On Safari
> everything else still works; the screen just dims as usual.

## Filling a recipe in with AI

If you have an API key for Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, MealMap can read a recipe for you.

1. **Settings → ✨ AI assistant.** Choose a provider, paste your key, press **Test connection**.
2. In **Add / Edit meal**, paste either the recipe text or a **link to a recipe page** into
   **✨ Fill from a recipe**, and press **Auto-fill**.

It fills in the name, category, time, servings, description, ingredients, steps, the photo, a link
back to the original, and nutrition where the page actually states it — it is told never to invent
nutrition figures.

Two things worth knowing:

- **It writes the recipe in whatever language MealMap is set to.** An English page becomes a
  Slovenian recipe if you are using MealMap in Slovenian — which matters, because that is what lets
  your pantry match it.
- **It never quietly overwrites what you typed.** Anything you had already filled in is kept, and it
  tells you what it kept. **Use imported instead** takes the AI's version; **Undo** puts everything
  back.

Your key is stored only in this browser, sent only to the provider you chose, and is never included
in exports or sync. You pay the provider for what you use. To remove it, clear the key box and click
away.

## Settings

- **Language** — English, Slovenian, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese.
- **Units** — metric or imperial. This only changes how amounts are *shown*, so switching back and
  forth never damages a recipe.
- **AI assistant** — provider, key, model.
- **Data & sharing** — export, import, and the two syncing options below.

## Keeping your data safe

**Your recipes live in this browser and nowhere else.** There is no account and no server. That is
good for privacy, but it means clearing your browsing data, switching browser, or moving to a new
computer will start you over unless you have a copy.

Three ways to keep one, easiest first:

**1. Export a file.** *Settings → ⬇ Export JSON.* Keep it somewhere safe; **⬆ Import JSON** brings it
back — that is the one that keeps everything exactly.

**⬇ Export Excel** makes a readable copy, and **⬆ Import Excel** reads one back in. That is the way
to edit in bulk: export the workbook, paste in thirty recipes or fix a hundred ingredient lines in
the spreadsheet, and import it again. Two things do not survive the trip, because a spreadsheet
cannot hold them: **photos you uploaded** (linked photos are fine) and **batch-cooking links** —
those days come back as ordinary planned meals. Ingredients and steps live in one cell each,
separated by ` | `. Both kinds of import **replace** what is in the app, so the last file wins.

**2. Keep a file up to date** *(Chrome, Edge or Opera on a computer)*. Press **📄 Choose a file** and
pick a file inside a folder your cloud already syncs — your OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive or
iCloud folder. Tick **Keep it up to date automatically** and MealMap saves into that file as you
work, while your existing cloud app syncs it to your other machines. Nothing to sign up for.

> Choosing a file that already has recipes in it will **not** overwrite them. MealMap tells you what
> is in there and lets you choose: **⬇ Load now** to use it, or **⬆ Save now** to replace it.

**3. OneDrive live sync.** Real sharing between several people, but it needs one-time setup — see
[`ONEDRIVE-SETUP.md`](ONEDRIVE-SETUP.md).

> With either syncing option, **the last save wins**. If two people change the same thing at the same
> moment, one of the changes is lost. Fine for a household; not a database.

## When something looks wrong

**An ingredient I have is not badged.** The pantry name and the recipe wording may be too different —
try renaming the pantry item to the plain word (`Flour`, not `Plain white flour, organic`). Compound
ingredients are matched whole, so *Salt & black pepper* counts as covered if you only have salt.

**The shopping list did not add two amounts together.** It only adds up amounts it is confident
about — the same kind of measure, or the same unit word. Cloves plus a head of garlic stay separate
on purpose.

**"＋ From plan" added nothing.** Either nothing is scheduled in that range, or your pantry already
covers it all. The line under the button says which.

**The screen still dims in cook mode.** Safari does not support keeping it awake. Everything else in
cook mode works.

**I cannot see "Keep a file up to date".** It needs Chrome, Edge or Opera on a computer. On other
browsers, use Export / Import or OneDrive instead.

**Auto-fill says it could not read the recipe.** Some pages block being read. Copy the recipe text
itself and paste that in instead of the link.

**I lost everything.** If you have an exported file, **⬆ Import JSON** restores it. If you had linked
a file, press **⬇ Load now**. If neither — there is nothing to recover from, which is why the
Settings panel says so up front.
